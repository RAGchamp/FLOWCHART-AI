How This Tool Came to Be

It started with a single diagram. While learning about AI, I came across a complex flow chart: curved arc bands, Bézier ribbons, arrows, icons, and labels layered on top of each other. I wanted to replicate it, but not as a copy. I wanted the same shape with my own story in it. Every label, every word, and every color had to be mine to change freely, while the structure and geometry of the diagram stayed exactly the same.

My first thought was simple: I would hand the picture to Claude and it would recreate it for me. I uploaded the image, described what I wanted, and waited for a faithful, editable version.

It didn't work. I tried again with more detailed prompts, then by breaking the diagram into pieces, then by describing the shapes in words. Each attempt came close in places and drifted in others. Arcs bent the wrong way, bands lost their proportions, and elements slipped out of alignment. After many unsuccessful attempts, I learned something more useful than any single diagram: as of October 2026, a model on its own can't reliably replicate a complex flow chart image. It needs a harness, a purpose-built tool around it that gives it real shapes to work with, precise coordinates, and a structure it can edit without guessing. Maybe a future model will do it from a picture alone. For now, the tool makes the difference.

That realization changed the project. Instead of asking Claude to draw the diagram, I started building the tool with Claude's help. The result is Flow Chart Builder: you trace over a reference image with arcs, Bézier bands, arrows, circles, icons, and text, then save the whole diagram as JSON. Because the diagram is data, you can change any label or color whenever you like, and the shape stays exactly where you put it.

Where This Is Heading

Flow Chart Builder is a work in progress, and I plan to keep improving it. My goal is to add more automation features so that anyone can build complex flow diagrams quickly, spending less time placing shapes by hand and more time on the message the diagram is meant to tell.

If you have ideas, feature requests, or a diagram that pushes the tool to its limits, I'd love to hear from you. Reach out on LinkedIn or by email.
