For each step it shows what you do and what the app does behind the scenes, with a diagram of the idea. The last part explains how the app is built: one HTML file that draws with SVG and keeps the whole diagram as a list of shapes that saves straight to JSON.

1. The App at a Glance

PowerPoint, Excel and Publisher are good at boxes and straight connectors but poor at thick curved ribbons that loop, cross over each other and carry text along the curve. The Flow Chart App is built for exactly that. You draw rough curved bands, let Snap it join them into one smooth ribbon, then cut the ribbon into colored, labelled pieces by dropping arrows on it.

It is one HTML file (App\index.html) with no install, no server and no internet needed. Double-click it to open it in Chrome or Edge. Your work is saved as a small JSON file that you can load back later.

The building blocks
  • Band: a thick curved ribbon, either an Arc (part of a circle) or a Bézier (a free S-curve).
  • Snapped figure: bands joined by Snap it into one continuous ribbon.
  • Piece: the part of a snapped figure between two arrows. Each piece has its own color and text.
  • Arrow: a chevron that marks the flow and cuts the figure into pieces.
  • Text, Circle, Icon, Title: labels, numbered badges, icon + label, heading + subtitle.

3. Drawing Arc Bands

An arc band is a thick slice of a ring. Rings and loops are made from arcs, either one big arc with a gap or several arcs end to end (for example CURATE, COLLECT, VALIDATE, TRANSFORM around one center).

Left: click 1 sets the center, click 2 the radius and start, click 3 the end. Right: the handles of a selected arc.
STEP 2 — Three clicks: center, start, end
TOOLS.arc→previewArc()→finishCreate()
  1. Click Arc (or press A).
  2. Click 1: the center. It snaps to the center of another arc or circle within 12 screen pixels, so ring segments share one center.
  3. Click 2: the radius and start angle. It snaps to the end of another band.
  4. Move the mouse around the center; the sweep follows in either direction, up to almost a full circle (359.9°). Click 3 sets the end.

The new arc gets the last color you used and a thickness of 40 (less for a small radius), and its text direction is chosen so labels are not upside-down. The app then switches back to Select; hold Shift to keep drawing arcs. Esc cancels.

STEP 3 — Reshape with handles or exact numbers
drawSelection()→applyHandle()
  • Center (light blue): moves the arc.
  • Start / end (white, on the ends): change the angles.
  • Radius (white, middle of the band): makes the ring bigger or smaller.
  • Thickness (orange, outer edge): makes the band wider or narrower.
  • Drag the band itself to move it.

In the panel: fill color (with swatches), thickness, opacity, text, text color, font size, bold, Align / Position, Flip text, and the exact center, radius and angles (degrees clockwise from 3 o'clock).

4. Drawing Bézier Bands (S-Curves)

Left: white end points, filled blue control points with dashed guides, orange thickness handle. Right: a new Bézier started on an arc's end flows on from it.
STEP 4 — Start, end, then bend
TOOLS.bezier→bezierFrom()
  1. Click Bézier (or B).
  2. Click the start point, then the end point. Or press, drag and release in one move.
  3. The band starts as a gentle S. Drag the filled blue control points to bend it into swoops, S-shapes and crossovers.

Continuing a band smoothly: if you start (or end) on the end of another band, the point snaps to it and the control point is lined up with that band's direction, so the two flow into each other without a kink.

Dragging an end point also drags its control point, which keeps the curve's shape. The panel shows the four points (P0 start, P1 and P2 controls, P3 end) as exact numbers.

6. Snap it: One Continuous Figure

Hand-drawn bands rarely meet exactly: there are small gaps, overlaps and places where one band crosses another's end. Snap it (top bar, or S) closes all of them and turns each connected chain of bands into a single snapped figure in one color. From then on the figure behaves as one ribbon.

Snap it closes the gap, trims the overlap and turns the chain into one figure. It takes the first band's color.
What Snap it does, step by step
The three rules Snap it uses at every join.
SNAP 1 — Collect the bands and set a tolerance
snapIt()

Every arc, Bézier and open snapped figure on the canvas is collected (a figure that is already a closed loop has no ends to join). The join tolerance is 3 × the thickest band: with 40-thick bands, ends up to 120 units apart can be joined.

SNAP 2 — Pair up band ends that face each other
snapIt()→shapePoint()→shapeTangent()

Each band has two ends, each with an "outward" direction. Every pair of ends within the tolerance is scored by distance, plus a penalty when their directions don't continue each other. Pairs that point the same way (a hairpin, direction match above 0.7) are rejected. A band's two ends can be joined to close a ring, but joining to another band is preferred. The best-scoring pairs are accepted first, and each end is used once.

SNAP 3 — Walk the joins into chains

Starting from a free end, the app follows join after join and records each band and the direction it is travelled in (some bands are travelled backwards). Bands left over after all open chains are walked form closed loops. Each chain becomes one figure.

SNAP 4 — Build one smooth center line
sampleStrip()→joinPoint()→bridgePts()→resample()
  • Each band's center line is sampled into points about 3 units apart.
  • At every join the app looks at the last 2.5 × thickness of one band and the first 2.5 × thickness of the next, and finds the two closest points. For an overlap or crossing, that is where the bands meet, and the extra length is trimmed. For a gap, it is the two ends.
  • It steps back about half a thickness on both sides and fills the space with a smooth cubic curve that leaves one band and enters the next in their own directions.
  • Loops are closed the same way, end to start. The result is evened out to one point every 3 units.
SNAP 5 — Replace the bands, keep the arrows

The old bands are removed and the figure takes their place in the stacking order, so text and icons stay on top. It gets the first band's color and the thickest band's thickness. Any arrows already on the bands come back as cuts in the same places; if most of them pointed against the figure's direction, Reverse arrows is switched on. A message reports how many bands, figures and gaps were handled. The whole snap is one Ctrl+Z step.

Good to know
  • Snap it joins all bands on the canvas. Ends farther apart than the tolerance, or pointing back into each other, are left as open ends.
  • Text on the original bands is not carried over, because the figure starts as one color.
  • A snapped figure can be moved, recolored and split, but it has no curve handles. To reshape it, press Ctrl+Z, adjust the bands and snap again. New bands drawn next to a figure are joined into it the next time you click Snap it.

7. Arrows: Splitting the Figure into Pieces

Each click with the Arrow tool cuts the figure; the chevron takes the color of the piece it comes from.
STEP 5 — Click wherever an arrow belongs
TOOLS.arrow→findBandNear()→insertCut()
  1. Click Arrow (or R) and move over the figure. A faded chevron shows where the arrow will go and which way it points.
  2. Click. A chevron appears pointing along the flow, and the figure is cut there: the part after the arrow becomes a new piece and is selected, ready to recolor.
  3. Keep clicking to add more. On a snapped figure the Arrow tool stays active.

Near an open end of the figure the click adds an end arrow instead of a cut. A click within 8 units of an existing arrow is refused with a message.

The Arrow tool on the three kinds of target

Empty canvasA free arrow: press, drag to aim, release. A handle on its tip rotates it later.

8. Editing a Piece: Color, Text, Alignment, Arrow Style

STEP 6 — Click a piece, then use "This piece"
buildPanel()→data-prop="pieces.N.fill"

With Select (V), click a piece or its arrow. The selected piece gets a dashed outline, and the panel's This piece section edits only that piece:

  • Fill: color picker, hex code, or one of 10 swatches.
  • Text, Text color, Font size, Bold.
  • Align and Position, Flip text (see below).
  • Remove arrow before / after: merge with the neighbor.

Text runs along the middle of the piece, is turned automatically so it reads upright, and shrinks if it does not fit.

When another band crosses the middle of a piece, pin the label to the start or end edge.
STEP 7 — Move hidden text to an edge
addLabel()→fitTexts()
  • Center (use Position): the label sits at the Position slider (0–100 % along the piece).
  • Start edge / End edge: the label is pinned to that end of the piece, in the direction of flow, leaving room for the arrow tip or barbs. The Position slider is hidden.
  • Flip text turns the label the other way round if the automatic choice is wrong.

9. Which Piece Is on Top

Where a figure crosses itself, you choose which piece runs over the other.
STEP 8 — Bring a piece to the front
reorder()→renderPath()

Where a figure crosses itself, the piece drawn later normally covers the earlier one. To choose which is on top, click the hidden piece and press Bring piece to front (or Send piece to back) at the bottom of the panel.

Each piece remembers its own layer, which is saved in the JSON. An arrow between two pieces is drawn at the higher of their layers, so its tip stays visible. Bring piece to front also lifts the whole figure above other shapes; Send piece to back only reorders inside the figure.

11. Tracing a Picture of a Flow Chart (by hand or with AI)

The picture sits between the paper and your shapes, so you draw over it without ever selecting it.
STEP 0 — (Optional) copy an existing picture
openTrace()→#traceImg→clearTrace()
  1. Click Trace image and choose a picture or photo of a flow chart.
  2. It appears faded behind everything, fitted inside the canvas without stretching, and the view zooms to show it all.
  3. Draw arcs and Béziers over its bands. Clicks pass through the picture, so it never gets selected or moved.
  4. Change the Visibility slider in the panel (with nothing selected) to fade it more or less.
  5. When done, click Clear image. The canvas is white again and your drawing stays.

To use the whole page, set the canvas size to the picture's shape first (for example 1600 × 1200 for a 4:3 photo).

The tracing picture is a drawing aid only. It is not saved in the JSON file and is not restored when the page is reopened.

Let the AI trace it

Instead of drawing over the picture yourself, click Trace it. FlowTrace, a small vision program that runs on your own computer, reads the picture and draws it as normal app shapes: one snapped figure cut into colored, labelled pieces, plus icons with names, numbered badges and the title. Everything stays editable, and the whole trace is one Ctrl+Z step.

AI 1 — Start FlowTrace (once per session)

Double-click AI\start-flowtrace.bat in the FLOW-CHART-APP folder and leave its window open. It only listens on this computer; no picture leaves it. With nothing selected, the panel's AI trace box shows a green dot when it is ready, or a red dot with Check again if it is not running.

It needs Python with the packages in AI\requirements.txt, and Tesseract OCR to read text. Without Tesseract it still draws the ribbons, but no labels.

AI 2 — Trace it: corners first
aiStart()→drawCorners()→aiRun()
  1. Upload the picture with Trace image, then click Trace it (top bar or panel, or press Shift+T).
  2. Four orange corner handles appear, already placed around the diagram. Drag them onto the diagram's corners. Leave out side text, slide title bars and the screen's edge. This also straightens a photo taken at an angle.
  3. Press Enter or click Trace now. Esc cancels. Tracing takes about 5–20 seconds, and Cancel stops it.
  4. If the canvas already has shapes, choose Add to drawing or Replace drawing.

The faded picture is swapped for the straightened one, so the result lines up with it. Click Trace it again to start over from the original photo with the same corners.

AI 3 — Review the result
aiApply()→aiReviewPanel()

The panel shows AI trace result: what was found, and a Needs a look list of uncertain items (labels that may be misread, unclear colors, crossings where it could not tell which ribbon is on top). They are also circled in orange on the canvas. Click an item to select that piece, then fix it with the usual tools (section 8; for crossings, Bring piece to front, section 9).

Accept clears the orange marks. Undo trace (or Ctrl+Z) removes the whole trace.

14. The App's Design

One file, no dependencies

The whole app is App\index.html: about 2,260 lines holding the CSS, the page layout and one <script>. It uses no libraries, no build step and no server, and it works offline. The script is split into clearly marked sections (listed in the table at the end of this section).

It draws with SVG rather than an HTML <canvas>. Every shape is a real page element, so the browser does click detection for free, text can follow a curve natively (<textPath>), and drawings stay sharp at any zoom.

Every change goes into one list of shapes; the screen is always redrawn from that list.
DESIGN 1 — One list of shapes is the whole diagram
state.shapes→render()→renderShape()

All drawing data lives in state.shapes, a plain list of objects. The SVG on screen is never the source of truth: after every change render() clears the shapes layer and redraws every shape from the list, sorted by its z (stacking) number. This makes the rest simple:

  • Save is just writing the list to JSON, and Load is reading it back and calling render().
  • Undo keeps up to 200 snapshots of the list; going back restores one and redraws.
  • Autosave writes the same JSON to the browser's local storage.
The SVG is built in layers, bottom to top, inside one group that pans and zooms.
DESIGN 2 — The SVG is built in layers
#world→#paper→#traceImg→#gridRect→#shapesLayer→#overlayLayer

One #world group carries the pan and zoom (translate(x y) scale(k)). The overlay is redrawn on its own while the mouse moves, so hovering never rebuilds the diagram, and its elements ignore clicks except the handles.

DESIGN 3 — Tools are small objects that receive the mouse
TOOLS→pointerdown / move / up→toWorld()

Each tool (select, arc, bezier, arrow, text, circle, item, title) is an object with down, move, up and a hint. The canvas forwards mouse events to the active tool after converting screen pixels to canvas units with toWorld(), so drawing works the same at any zoom. A tool in the middle of a drawing keeps its progress in creating; finishing calls finishCreate(), which adds the shape, selects it and records an undo step.

How a snapped figure is stored: points along its center line, arrows as positions from 0 to 1, pieces in between.
DESIGN 4 — The geometry behind the bands
shapePoint()→shapeTangent()→pathGeo()
  • Arc: the point at angle θ is (cx + r·cos θ, cy + r·sin θ); the direction of travel is (−sin θ, cos θ), flipped for a counter-clockwise sweep. The band is drawn as a closed outline: outer arc at r + t/2, inner arc back at r − t/2.
  • Bézier: B(t) = (1−t)³P0 + 3(1−t)²t·P1 + 3(1−t)t²·P2 + t³P3, drawn as a thick stroke. Its slope B′(t) gives arrow directions.
  • Snapped figure: a list of center-line points with the running length at each point. A position on it is a fraction from 0 to 1 of its length; arrows (cuts) are stored as these fractions. Pieces are the stretches between cuts; on a closed loop the last piece wraps round to the first cut.
  • Arrowheads are built from a point and a direction by chevronPoints() and drawn by drawHead().
DESIGN 5 — The panel is generated and bound by name
buildPanel()→applyInput()→commit()

buildPanel() writes the panel for whatever is selected. Each input names the setting it controls in data-prop, for example fill, p1.0 (a Bézier's P1 x) or pieces.2.textAlign (piece 3's alignment). Typing updates the shape and redraws live; finishing a change (the change event) records one undo step.

Tips
  • Draw roughly, then let Snap it clean up; it is easier than placing every join exactly.
  • Choose the arrow style before fine-tuning arrow sizes; changing the style resets them.
  • Pick a swatch first: new arcs, badges and icons use your most recent color.
  • Zoom in for small adjustments; snapping and handles are measured in screen pixels.
Limits
  • A snapped figure has no curve handles. Undo the snap, reshape the bands, and snap again.
  • Text on a band is one line and shrinks to fit rather than wrapping.
  • Uploaded icon pictures are stored inside the JSON, which makes the file bigger (each is limited to 1.5 MB).
  • The tracing picture is not saved.
  • Trace it needs the FlowTrace service running on this computer (AI\start-flowtrace.bat). It knows ribbon diagrams, reads English labels, and can misread small or blurry text; check the Needs a look list.
  • In an exported PowerPoint file, a label on a tight curve is one text box per letter; to retype it, export with Labels: Straight or edit the letters.
